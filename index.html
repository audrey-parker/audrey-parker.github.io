01 / About

Working across scales to address a fast-moving climate challenge.

I am a PhD candidate in Civil and Environmental Engineering at MIT. My research centers on methane mitigation and the technical, economic, and market conditions that shape real-world deployment.

My work moves between dairy barns, catalyst reactors, atmospheric data, and carbon markets. Across these settings, I ask a consistent question: what does it take for a promising climate intervention to produce durable, quantifiable benefit?

I also work at the intersection of research and decision-making through the MIT Climate and Sustainability Consortium, climate-finance research, stakeholder workshops, and science-policy engagement.

02 / Research

From measurement to materials to markets.

An integrated research program for understanding whether climate solutions work, where they work, and how they can scale responsibly.

01

Methane measurement & mitigation

Field campaigns and modeling to characterize dilute methane sources, define realistic operating conditions, and quantify the net climate benefit of mitigation.

Field + systems
02

Catalytic materials

High-throughput reactor studies of copper zeolites, including activity, kinetics, durability, and resistance to environmental interferents.

Materials + kinetics
03

Climate finance & carbon markets

Portfolio-scale analysis connecting greenhouse-gas credits with near- and long-term temperature outcomes, cost effectiveness, and implementation risk.

Data + decisions
15years of satellite observations analyzed
10k+carbon-market projects evaluated
60+stakeholders convened
