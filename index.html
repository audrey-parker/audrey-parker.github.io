01 / About

Working across disciplines to address a fast-moving climate challenge.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Civil and Environmental Engineering at MIT. My research centers on methane mitigation and the technical, economic, and market conditions that shape real-world deployment.

My research integrates environmental chemistry, field measurements, and techno-economic modeling to develop and evaluate scalable technologies for mitigating methane emissions from dilute sources. Beyond technological feasibility, I investigate the intersection of climate science, greenhouse gas accounting, and carbon markets to identify systemic barriers and opportunities for accelerating the deployment of high-impact climate solutions.

02 / Research

From measurement to materials to markets.

Methane measurement & mitigation

I have led field campaigns at New England dairy farms, mapping the spatial and temporal distribution of methane emissions under real-world operating conditions. I used these measurements to build a model that predicts methane concentrations from known operating parameters and quantifies the net climate benefit of deploying thermal catalytic oxidation.

Atmospheric Chemistry Techno-Economic Analysis Stakeholder Engagement Analytical Chemistry

Catalytic materials

Utilizing my background in materials science and engineering, I investigate copper-doped zeolite catalysts for the oxidation of dilute methane emissions from sources such as dairy barns and coal mine ventilation systems. My research combines catalyst synthesis, experimental reactor studies, and advanced materials characterization to understand how catalyst composition and structure influence methane oxidation efficiency and long-term stability. Ultimately, this work aims to identify the mechanisms governing catalytic activity and deactivation to inform the development of scalable and cost-effective methane mitigation technologies.

Materials Science Chemical Engineering Atmospheric Chemistry Environmental Engineering

Climate finance & carbon markets

My research examines the role of voluntary carbon markets (VCMs) in accelerating climate mitigation, with a particular focus on the underutilization of methane and other short-lived climate pollutants. Through interdisciplinary collaboration with MIT Sloan and engagement with corporate stakeholders through the MIT Climate & Sustainability Consortium (MCSC), I integrate climate science, economic analysis, and business strategy to evaluate how carbon accounting frameworks and market incentives influence investment in greenhouse gas mitigation. By analyzing carbon credit data across more than 10,000 projects, I aim to identify opportunities to improve market mechanisms, mobilize corporate climate finance, and direct investment toward solutions that maximize near-term climate benefits.
